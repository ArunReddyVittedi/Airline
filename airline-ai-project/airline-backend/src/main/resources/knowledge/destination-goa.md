# Goa

Goa is a small coastal state on the west of India, known for its beaches, Portuguese era
churches and a slow pace that surprises first time visitors.

## When to go
November to February is the season: warm and dry, 22 to 32 degrees, and every beach shack
is open. This is also when fares and hotels are at their most expensive, and the last two
weeks of December sell out months ahead.

June to September is the monsoon. Rain is heavy, boat trips do not run, many shacks close
and swimming is genuinely unsafe because of the currents. It is beautiful, empty and cheap,
which suits some travellers and disappoints most.

March to May is humid and quiet, and prices sit between the two.

## North and south
North Goa around Baga, Calangute and Anjuna is busy, cheap and loud, with markets and
nightlife. South Goa around Palolem, Agonda and Colva is quiet and more expensive, and
better if the point of the trip is to do very little.

Distances look small on a map and take longer than expected. North to south is two hours by
road, so it is better to choose one end and stay there.

## Getting around
Renting a scooter is the normal way to travel and costs 400 to 600 rupees a day. An
international driving permit is required for visitors, and police checks are common.

## What catches people out
The nearest airport, GOI, is in the south of the state, so a hotel in north Goa is over an
hour from the terminal. Book an airport transfer in advance rather than arriving at
midnight and negotiating.
